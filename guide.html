HOW TO PLAY

The duel. Two death-gods duel across the Crossing, the battlefield where the dead pass over. Your Deity starts in play with Divinity 20. Reduce the enemy Deity's Divinity to zero and you win. Divinity damage is permanent — but Follower damage heals at Dawn, so the board resets every morning.

Setup. Each player brings a 60-card deck (max 3 copies of any card) and one Deity, placed face-up in play. Draw an opening hand of 5 cards; you may take one free mulligan (shuffle back, draw 5). The seat rule: nobody draws at their first Dawn, and the second player starts with +2 Obols.

Obols are the ferryman's coin — the game's only resource. Your treasury can never hold more than 12 Obols; unspent Obols stay in your treasury.

Your turn — six beats, same every turn

1. Dawn — Refresh all your cards. Followers heal fully. Draw 1 card — except at your first Dawn of the game, when no player draws.
2. The Toll — Gain Obols equal to the toll count: it starts at 2, rises by 1 each of your turns, and caps at 6.
3. The Descent — Followers waiting in your Underworld may return one Level deeper. Decide and pay their fares now, from the treasury the Toll just filled.
4. Summoning — Play Followers, Rites (one-shot rites of power), and Relics (persistent charms and gear), paying their Obol costs.
5. The Reckoning — Combat. Every Follower can strike the turn it is summoned — there is no summoning sickness. Each ready Follower may strike once: an enemy Follower, or the enemy Deity if the way is clear. The defender may interpose their own Followers to block. Damage equals Might, dealt against Health; dead Followers cross to their owner's Underworld.
6. Dusk — "When Dusk falls" effects trigger. Pass the turn.

Pierce (universal keyword). When a Piercing Follower's strike is blocked and the blocker dies, the excess damage carries through to the enemy Deity's Divinity. Blocking a Piercing strike with a weak body just delays the wound.

Descend (the Obsidian keyword). When a Follower with Descend dies, it is not gone: at The Descent on your next turn it may return one Level deeper — +1/+1 and an added line of text per Level, up to its printed Depth. Returning costs Obols equal to the Level it returns at; if you can't or won't pay, it waits in your Underworld for a later turn.

The five pantheon keywords

Drain — Vampyre Court. When this deals damage, your Deity gains that much Divinity.
Harvest — Reaper Choir. Whenever any Follower dies, its Harvest triggers (gain Obols, small effects). Reapers get paid by every death on the table.
Wrath — Fallen Host. When your Follower destroys an enemy, it takes a Judgment counter (+1 Might per counter). Punishment compounds.
Pact — The Loa. Cards with an optional extra cost (Divinity, a discard, an Obol debt) for a much stronger effect. Deals with the Baron are always available — and always priced.
Descend — Obsidian Court. Death is a staircase, and the ferryman charges by the step (see above).
Veiled — Universal. Cannot be chosen as a strike target until the start of your next turn.

The Game in One Sentence

Two death-gods duel across the Crossing (the battlefield where the dead pass over). Your Deity starts in play, mints Obols from the dead who pay the ferryman, and the game ends when a Deity's Divinity reaches zero.

Setup

  • Each player: a 60-card deck (Joe's call, 2026-10-20) + 1 Deity card, placed face-up in play. Max 3 copies of any card (starters are fixed lists).
  • Opening hand: 5 cards. One free mulligan (shuffle back, draw 5).
  • The second player starts with +2 Obols (the ferryman favors the patient — and pays for the wait in coin, since v0.4; see the v0.4 Changelog).
  • Nobody draws at their first Dawn — the first-turn draw is removed altogether (Joe's ruling, v0.3). Both players open with their 5 cards and play the first turn from hand; the Dawn draw begins on each player's second turn. (History: v0.1 had full draws and going first won ~2 of every 3 simulated games; v0.2 skipped only the first player's first draw and the seat edge fell to ~57%; v0.3 removed the first-turn draw for both players — Joe chose this over the alternative of giving the second player an extra card at their first Dawn — but with only +1 Obol the seat edge widened to ~61.5%, because the second player's draw was itself compensation. v0.4 keeps the no-draw rule and moves the compensation into coin: second player starts with +2 Obols, and the first-player win rate landed at 54.9% over 800 simulated games, inside the ~50–55% target set before the run. The seat rule is final: nobody draws at their first Dawn; the second player starts with 2 Obols.)

The Five Deities (roster v1 — all from ACTUAL dark mythology, per Joe's ruling 2026-10-20)

All Deities: Divinity 20 (Joe confirmed). Each grants its pantheon an aura and one signature rite.

  1. LILITH, Mother of the Night — Vampyre Court. First rebel of Mesopotamian and Jewish myth; mother of demons, the original blood-drinking night terror. Aura: your Followers have Drain. Signature: The First Hunger.
  2. THANATOS, the Iron-Hearted — Reaper Choir. Greek death itself — silent wings, a sword that takes a lock of hair from every soul he claims. Aura: whenever a Follower dies, gain 1 Obol (once per death). Signature: The Lock of Hair — claim what is owed.
  3. AZRAEL, the Veiled Angel — Fallen Host. The Angel of Death of Jewish and Islamic tradition, keeper of the book where every birth and death is written. Aura: your Wrath counters also grant +1 Health. Signature: The Book Closes — a name is struck from the living.
  4. BARON SAMEDI, Lord of the Cemetery — The Loa. Ghede father of the dead in Haitian Vodou: top hat, black tails, sunglasses with one lens out — one eye on the living, one on the dead. He digs the grave, and nothing crosses without his say. Aura: your Pacts cost 1 Obol less. Signature: The Grave Is Dug — a deal, signed in rum and earth. (Living tradition — treat with respect in art and copy; homage, never mockery.)
  5. ITZPAPALOTL, the Obsidian Butterfly — Obsidian Court. Aztec goddess of Tamoanchan, skeletal butterfly whose wings are obsidian blades. Aura: one Descent return each game is free of its return cost (one per game, total — Joe's ruling 2026-10-20). Signature: Wings of Knives.

Demoted, not deleted: Carmilla (literary, not mythology) becomes a named Vampyre follower under Lilith; Vesper and Malachar become named Fallen/Reaper cards; Baron Minuit becomes one of Samedi's Ghede lieutenants.

Balance Framework (Joe's rule: no broken deck, ever)

  • Power budget: a Follower's Might + Health ≤ 2 × its Obol cost + 2. Every keyword is priced against the same budget (Drain ≈ +2, Pierce ≈ +1–2 by Might, Descend = priced by Depth).
  • Pentagram counters (ratified by Joe, 2026-10-20): the counters run in 5-point star order — Lilith → Thanatos → Azrael → Samedi → Itzpapalotl. Each pantheon preys on the two across the star (Lilith > Thanatos, Samedi · Thanatos > Azrael, Itzpapalotl · Azrael > Lilith, Samedi · Samedi > Thanatos, Itzpapalotl · Itzpapalotl > Lilith, Azrael) and is pressured by its two neighbors — structural, not accidental. The wheel IS the tactical layer: deck choice is a bet on what the table brings.
  • Playtest gate (wheel bands, replaces the flat 45–55% gate): no starter ships unless the wheel holds its shape over the Crew's test games — each favored matchup lands at 57–63% for the predator (seat-averaged), each unfavored matchup mirrors at 37–43%, and mirror matches sit at ~50%. A deck is broken if it beats a deck it should lose to, or runs a favored edge far above band. If the wheel breaks, the deck changes — not the target.

Deckbuilding Horizon (Joe, ruled direction 2026-10-20; mandatory-two ruled 2026-10-21)

  • Starters stay mono-pantheon (already the design — new players learn one economy at a time, and the pilot ships clean).
  • RULED (Joe, 2026-10-21): when deckbuilding opens, every deck MUST run exactly TWO pantheons. There is no mono option in constructed play — each player picks 2 pantheons and 1 of their Deities to lead. That is 10 pairings × 2 leaders = 20 led hybrids as the constructed format, and the counter wheel becomes a deckbuilding meta: a hybrid carries both its pantheons' edges on the star.
  • RULED (Joe, 2026-10-20): each player uses only 1 Deity — its aura is the deck's only aura. Max-3-copies and the 60-card size carry over. Splash minimums, if any, remain open — under the current free-mix test convention a "two-pantheon" deck could still run 57/3, so a minimum presence for the second pantheon is a live structural question, not a settled one.
  • GUARDRAILS RULED (Joe, 2026-10-21) — the format runs behind two rails: 1. Aura scoping (CONSTRUCTED play only — see the format distinction below). A Deity's aura benefits only Followers of its own pantheon. Splash followers fight at their printed text — they do not feed on the lead Deity's aura (no Drain healing off foreign bodies, no aura coin for foreign deaths, no counter-growth or free returns from a foreign aura). Samedi's Pact discount needs no scoping clause: Pact exists only on Loa cards, so it is scoped by construction.
    • FORMAT DISTINCTION (Joe, ruled 2026-10-21): aura scoping is a rule of the constructed (mandatory-two) format. In starter-deck (precon) play, auras work exactly as printed on the roster — most importantly Thanatos's coin, which in the starters pays on any Follower death, as printed. The starters were tuned and verified on that literal reading (Mono Confirmation Report v1: all five decks in the 45–55 band), and applying any scoped reading to them breaks the wheel (Thanatos falls to 40.8) — scoping must never be applied to starter play. The constructed reading of the coin, stated in full: a Thanatos-led deck gains the Obol only when a Reaper-pantheon Follower it owns dies — the two-axis reading, retained after the Coin Variant Report v1 (2026-10-21) measured the pantheon-only alternative at ≤ +0.8 for Thanatos-led decks with no field-wide benefit. 2. Signature cap. A Deity's signature rite is capped at 1 copy in any constructed deck (the starters already print them at 1; the cap binds deckbuilding, where the stress test found signature rites run at 3 copies driving broken spell halves — Book Closes ×3 being the pattern case).
  • Evidence on record: Hybrid Stress Test v1 (2026-10-20, free mix vs the mono field) — the pillar breaks against mono: 10 of 20 hybrids broken, worst Lilith-led Azrael splash at 72.5%. Hybrid Field Test v1 (2026-10-21, 20×20 round-robin) — mandatory-two unguarded does not self-balance: only 8 of 20 viable, L-A dominant at 65.9, the {Lilith, Thanatos} pairing a trap in both directions. The guardrail PREVIEW in that test (scoping + signature cap) normalized most worst cells (aura scoping ≈ three-quarters of the effect) but left L-A posting ~70 against trap decks — a full 20×20 re-run under the adopted combo is the verification gate, and L-A may still need a specific lever. Splash minimums remain open and unruled.
  • Design consequence, noted now: today's cards are being built knowing they will cross-pollinate. The cross-pantheon engines to watch are the ones that stack economies — a Loa Pact paid for by Reaper Harvest Obols, Descend bodies feeding a Reaper Harvest twice (the death and the fare), Drain healing under Azrael's Judgment growth. The no-broken-deck watch doubles when hybrids open; the wheel gate will extend to hybrid archetypes in that phase.

Rulings — v0.2 (Joe, 2026-10-20)

  • Itzpapalotl's aura scope: her aura waives the fare of one Descent return per game, total — not one per Follower. (The v0.1 texts disagreed with each other; this is the ruling. Simulated effect of the alternative reading is in the v0.2 playtest report: per-Follower free returns were worth ~4.5 points of matchup swing.)
  • Call from the Deep / Smoking Mirror: a Follower card put into the Underworld without dying (e.g., milled by Smoking Mirror) is a legal target for Call from the Deep. It never entered play, so it counts as Level 0 and enters one Level deeper — at Level 1 if it has Descend, at its printed stats if it doesn't. Because it never died, putting it there triggers no Harvest, no Thanatos aura, and no Dawn Descent return — Call from the Deep (or The Silent Summons) is the only way back.

v0.5 Changelog (pentagram wheel ratified, Joe 2026-10-20)

  1. Counter structure, formalized: Joe ruled the five decks' counters run "like a 5-point star" — ratifying the pentagram map already drawn in Starter Deck Lists v0.2 (each deck preys on the two across the star, pressured by its two neighbors; the full prey map is now stated in the Balance Framework above).
  2. Playtest gate, re-scoped: the flat "every matchup 45–55%" gate from v0.1–v0.4 is REPLACED by wheel bands (favored 57–63%, unfavored 37–43%, mirror ~50%). Under the old gate a working counter wheel would have failed its own test; under the new one, "broken" means breaking the wheel's shape. Joe's balance-first intent is unchanged — no deck may beat the decks designed to beat it.
  3. Consequence for testing: all simulation through v0.4 covered a single pairing (Thanatos vs Itzpapalotl — a Thanatos-favored edge he was losing 65.4/34.6, i.e. both inverted and out of band). Testing moves to the full 10-pairing wheel matrix (plus mirrors) before any further card tuning; single-pairing fixes are not to be drawn from one edge again.
  4. Unchanged, deliberately: every rule, keyword, deity, and card carries over from v0.4 exactly. The seat rule stays closed (no first-Dawn draw; second player +2 Obols).

v0.3 Changelog (Joe's ruling, 2026-10-20)

  1. Seat compensation, simplified: the first-turn draw is removed altogether — neither player draws a card at their first Dawn. This replaces the v0.2 rule (only the first player skipped the draw) and supersedes the v0.2 report's proposed next lever (an extra card for the second player at their first Dawn), which Joe rejected in favor of removing the draw. The second player's +1 Obol is unchanged. Nothing else in the rules, the keywords, or any card changes in v0.3; the deck lists remain at v0.2. Simulated 2026-10-20 (800 games, Playtest Report v0.3): this change moved the seat edge the WRONG way — first-player win rate rose 56.8% → 61.5% and the matchup widened to Itzpapalotl 62.2%. The second player's first-Dawn draw was itself doing compensation work. This configuration was carried into one final, pre-agreed test as v0.4 (second player starts with 2 Obols instead of 1) — it did NOT revert to v0.2. See the v0.4 Changelog for the closing of the seat question.

v0.4 Changelog (seat question CLOSED, 2026-10-20)

  1. Seat compensation, final: the draw rule stays exactly as v0.3 wrote it — neither player draws a card at their first Dawn — and the second player's starting compensation rises from +1 to +2 Obols (the treasury holds 2 before the first Toll). Nothing else in the rules, the keywords, or any card changes in v0.4; the deck lists remain at v0.2.
  2. Why this closes it: v0.4 was run under a verdict rule agreed BEFORE the batch was simulated: if the first-player win rate landed in ~50–55%, Joe's clean no-draw rule stands with coin compensation; if it stayed above 55%, the draw itself was the compensation and v0.2's seat rule would be restored instead. Result (800 games, Playtest Report v0.4): first player 54.9% — inside the window. The seat question is closed. The margin is recorded honestly: the two batches read 56.2% and 53.5%, so the seat sits at the fair end of the target, not the middle of it.
  3. What the seat fix did and didn't do: within each deck, the first/second gap compressed to 9.7 points — the smallest of any version (v0.2: 13.4, v0.3: 23.0) — so as a mechanism, the seat is the fairest it has ever been. But the matchup moved the other way: Itzpapalotl 65.4% / Thanatos 34.6%, further outside the 45–55% band, because a starting Obol is worth roughly 2.7× more to the Obol-gated Obsidian deck than to card-hungry Thanatos. That is no longer a seat problem; it is a card-tuning problem. The next lever is named in Playtest Report v0.4 (The Ferryman's Lantern redesign) and is deliberately NOT applied here.

v0.2 Changelog (from the v0.1 playtest analyses, approved by Joe 2026-10-20)

  1. Seat compensation: the first player now skips the draw at their first Dawn (the Monte Carlo report's #1-ranked fix; the second player's +1 Obol is unchanged).
  2. Descend timing: Descent returns are decided and paid at a new step, The Descent, after the Toll — no longer at Dawn. Fare amounts unchanged (fare = the Level returned at).
  3. Harvest caps: Pale Accountant, Choir of Final Breaths (and Charon, moot — see 4) now trigger once per turn, matching Oarsman of the Styx / The Harvester of Nations.
  4. Charon, the Ferryman: his Harvest rider is removed; he keeps his summon discount exactly as printed otherwise.
  5. Offering of Hearts: pays the sacrificed Follower's cost minus 2 Obols.
  6. Relic costs: The Ferryman's Lantern 3 → 2 Obols; Charon's Obol Pouch 2 → 1 Obol.
  7. Rules text: The Lock of Hair now targets "a Follower that was dealt damage this turn or is below full Health"; Veiled is defined as "until the start of your next turn" (above).
  8. Unchanged, deliberately: the 12-Obol treasury cap, Descend fare pricing, Marigold Altar's text (watch item), and all Obsidian top-end stat lines.

THE FIVE DECKS

Five starter decks, five economies — each one mono-pantheon, 60 cards, built around one Deity. The counters between them run in pentagram star order — Lilith → Thanatos → Azrael → Samedi → Itzpapalotl — and each deck preys on the two across the star while its two neighbors pressure it. Deck choice is a bet on what the table brings.

Aura: One Descent return each game is free of its return cost.

Keyword: Descend · Signature rite: Wings of Knives

36 Followers · 14 Rites · 10 Relics · 60 cards

Aztec goddess of Tamoanchan, a skeletal butterfly whose wings are obsidian blades. Her court carries Descend: when one of her Followers dies it is not gone — at The Descent on your next turn it may return one Level deeper (+1/+1 and an added line of text per Level, up to its printed Depth), for a fare in Obols equal to the Level it returns at. Her aura waives the fare of one Descent return each game. Trade aggressively, let the Marigold Altar pay for the climb back, and every returned body is a bigger problem than the one they just solved. Her signature rite, Wings of Knives, deals 3 damage to each enemy Follower and sends your own dead back deeper.
